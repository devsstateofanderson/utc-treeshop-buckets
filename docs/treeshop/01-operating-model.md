# UTC–TreeShop–Buckets operating model

Status: founder working model, September 2026. The legal entity and DBA structure described here are intended structure until registration and ownership documents establish them.

## Purpose

TreeShop installs a measurable operating system in a service company, manages agreed critical functions, and improves decisions and execution using real operating data. Buckets is the flagship product that makes costs, prices, goals, work, and results visible. UTC is the ownership and organization layer above TreeShop and future brands.

## Organization

| Layer | Role | Day-to-day identity |
|---|---|---|
| UTC — Unnamed Technical Company | Intended parent entity for intellectual property, capital allocation, shared technology, and future brands | Rarely customer-facing |
| TreeShop | Public-facing brand and operating team serving tree services and adjacent service businesses | Employees and customers say “TreeShop” |
| Buckets | Product line owned by UTC and delivered and operated through TreeShop | The client sees Buckets as part of TreeShop's service |
| Client company | Owns its customers, employees, domain, accounts, data, and operating decisions delegated to TreeShop | Continues to perform its real-world service |

Mr. J. Anderson is the sole owner of UTC, TreeShop, and Buckets. He is the product designer, lead operator, and public face of the TreeShop service. Alexander Satoski owns and manages Sacred Tree Service, the first client company. Sacred Tree Service hired TreeShop and Mr. Anderson to improve operations, install Buckets, and scale its brand. Technical staff and AI tools execute defined work under the applicable owner's authority. TreeShop's value comes from operating judgment, execution, and accountability; Buckets makes the method repeatable.

## What each system owns

| System | Record or action |
|---|---|
| Jobber | Frontline customer, quote, schedule, visit, invoice, and field execution records where the client uses Jobber |
| Buckets today | Company costs, project estimates, pricing assumptions, margin, packages, loadouts, and estimate-versus-actual review |
| TreeShop operating record | Goals, decisions, budgets, owners, due dates, approvals, work evidence, and results; initially documented manually, then built into Buckets Pro |
| Website and marketing accounts | Public identity, leads, campaigns, spend, and acquisition performance; linked to the operating record |

One number should have one named source. Buckets may calculate a metric from Jobber data, but it should retain the source period and calculation method. Where Jobber and Buckets disagree, the discrepancy becomes a task with an owner rather than a silent overwrite.

## The operating loop

1. **Baseline:** Capture the current facts, source files, definitions, and confidence level. Record missing or unreliable inputs.
2. **Intent:** State the desired business outcome, why it matters, constraints, budget, owner, and date for review.
3. **Plan:** Turn intent into a short sequence of actions. Each action has a named owner and proof of completion.
4. **Execute:** TreeShop and the client's team perform their agreed actions. External changes follow the client's approval rules.
5. **Measure:** Compare results with the baseline and forecast. Log what changed and what remains unknown.
6. **Decide again:** Keep, adjust, or stop the initiative. Feed reusable lessons into Buckets and the TreeShop playbook.

The first manual version can live in a company-specific operating log. The future Buckets Pro representation is: **intent → owner → action → budget → deadline → evidence → result**. The software should encode this loop only after live use shows which fields and decisions recur.

## Responsibility and authority

- The Sacred Tree client owner approves Sacred Tree strategy, spending limits, account access, public claims, hiring/firing, and material changes to Sacred Tree customer pricing unless authority is explicitly delegated.
- Mr. Anderson owns UTC, TreeShop, and Buckets strategy, product direction, service design, implementation standards, and the shared product roadmap.
- TreeShop owns the agreed operating cadence, analysis, recommendations, implementation work, and a record of changes and results for each client.
- The field team owns safe service delivery and accurate frontline records. TreeShop improves the tools around that team rather than replacing its judgment.
- AI tools may draft, analyze, reconcile, and implement within granted access. A named human remains accountable for consequential external actions.

## Product trajectory

**Now:** Buckets v1.1 is a local Mac pricing application with six cost buckets, project snapshots, target-margin pricing, actuals, packages, loadouts, company records, and JSON transfer. It has no Jobber connection, multi-company administration, goal tracker, marketing calendar, or agent control panel today.

**Next:** Build read-only Jobber data ingestion, a baseline/scorecard, an operating log, and dependable backup/restore around the first client's real workflow. Add write-back only after the data mapping and approval process are proven.

**After five to ten companies:** Hire technically proficient operators, standardize deployment, add multi-company administration and tenant separation, and decide which work moves from dedicated Macs to a server. The first ten installations are the evidence for that architecture.

**Long horizon:** Buckets may support a more objective scoring model for tree work and a dedicated field device. These are research directions; current client promises use the capabilities above.

## Product decision rule

Prioritize a change when it makes a live decision more accurate, completes important work faster, reduces repeated labor, improves accountability, or makes the next installation easier. Record the specific client bottleneck and expected result before starting development. Review the result after use.
