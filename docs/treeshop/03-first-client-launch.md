# First-client launch plan

Status: working plan for the first live TreeShop installation, assumed to be Sacred Tree Service. Launch day puts the control desk into use. The first 30 days establish a reliable baseline and improvement rhythm.

## Before the reset: owner session

Confirm the company's current name, domain, primary owner, device serial, purchase/ownership record, and who may authorize spending and public changes. Inventory the MacBook's personal photos, documents, browser profiles, messages, keys, and Apple Account connection. The owner verifies a restorable copy of anything personal they want to keep before the device is erased. Record the reset approval and keep the owner available for Apple Account and Activation Lock prompts.

Inventory, by owner and current login method: Google Workspace, domain registrar/DNS, Jobber, website host or CMS, Google Business Profile, Search Console, Analytics, Ads, Meta, Canva, GitHub, Vercel or other host actually in use, Anthropic, and OpenAI. Create or assign a company-controlled operator identity such as `devtech@<company-domain>` where appropriate. Do not replace the owner's recovery access with the operator account.

Collect source documents before configuration: current P&L, payroll and burden evidence, employee roster and wage rates, equipment inventory and financing, insurance, major vendor/sub rates, overhead bills, existing website/marketing spend, Jobber exports, and at least a small sample of completed jobs with estimated and actual hours. Each source gets a date and a person who can confirm it.

## Launch day: controlled cutover

| Order | Work | Proof at completion |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Freeze a copy of the current account/device inventory and backup the owner's personal data | Owner confirms personal recovery path |
| 2 | Create the operator identity, recovery method, and access register | Owner retains admin/recovery access; operator can receive mail |
| 3 | Erase and configure the MacBook using the [device SOP](04-company-macbook-sop.md) | Fresh company user, encryption, updates, backup, owner recovery recorded |
| 4 | Sign into only the approved company systems and install the current Buckets release | Each login tested; Buckets opens on a company store |
| 5 | Preserve the existing Buckets store/export and import or merge only the chosen baseline | Opening row counts and settings recorded; rollback copy exists |
| 6 | Enter the smallest validated cost set: people, primary equipment, overhead, disposal, main subcontractors | One real project can be priced with source-tagged assumptions |
| 7 | Establish the operating log and first scoreboard | Owner can see current priorities, owners, unknowns, and next review date |
| 8 | Confirm the next business day workflow with the owner and one frontline user | A quote can be made and transferred to Jobber without guesswork about who acts next |

The public rebrand or “one-day hackathon” can be filmed and marketed, but publication, DNS moves, ads, and customer messages each have their own approval and rollback point. The business continues serving customers during the cutover. A first-day claim is “the new operating system is live,” with the initial data confidence stated plainly.

## Days 2–7: finish the baseline

- Validate the cost model line by line with the owner or source document. Replace placeholder rates and remove irrelevant catalog rows from the active estimating workflow.
- Reconcile at least five recent jobs between Jobber and Buckets. Note missing clock-outs, missing job attribution, and assumptions about travel, dump runs, and crew hours.
- Capture the current lead funnel, quote conversion, average response time if measurable, website leads, and marketing spend by channel.
- Select three operating priorities: one pricing/accuracy item, one sales or lead-handling item, and one administrative or digital foundation item.
- Complete a backup and restore test to a separate location. Record the date and responsible person.

## Days 8–30: prove the rhythm

Price new work through Buckets; keep Jobber as the frontline execution record. Review completed jobs for estimate-versus-actual variance each week. Hold a short owner review each week with decisions, budget changes, and the next actions recorded. Close the month with a baseline report and a first change report: starting point, actions taken, observed movement, unresolved data, and next month's plan.

## Launch acceptance

The first installation is operational when the company owns the device and core accounts, TreeShop can access its approved systems, Buckets can price a real job from documented inputs, the owner can see the first scorecard and work queue, and a usable backup and exit path exist. Accurate year-over-year conclusions are a later milestone.
